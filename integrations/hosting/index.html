For Hosting & Infrastructure

Hosting billing is not ordinary e-commerce

A shop takes a payment once. A hosting company takes the same payment every month, from customers in countries it has never visited, in a business category that half of the payment industry files under elevated risk. Those three facts turn payments from a checkout feature into an operational dependency — and they are why hosting, VPN, domain and managed-service businesses outgrow a single provider earlier than almost anyone else.

Renewals fail quietly

A renewal is a merchant-initiated charge against a stored agreement, with nobody at the keyboard. Plenty of gateways take the first payment perfectly and cannot do the second one at all, so the invoice sits unpaid, dunning runs, and a happy customer is suspended for a reason neither of you chose. The diagnosis is in why WHMCS recurring payments fail.

Your customers are everywhere

Infrastructure sells across borders by default. A customer in Lagos, São Paulo or Jakarta who cannot pay with the method they actually use does not switch methods — they close the tab. One provider is strong in some of those markets and absent in the rest, which is the whole content of why cards alone lose sales abroad.

You are a risk category

Hosting, VPN and domain resale attract holds, reserves and account reviews that a clothing store never sees. It is not a reflection on your business; it is how the category is underwritten. With one provider, a review stops every payment you take until it clears. With several already connected, it stops one of them.

Fraud arrives at signup

Cheap plans and instant provisioning make a hosting signup form an ideal place to validate stolen cards, and the bill for that lands on you as declines, fees and eventually a merchant review. Attempt limits, distinct-card limits and rules that react to repeated rejections are the controls that matter — see card testing on signup forms.

The providers this industry actually asks for

Cards are the floor, not the answer. These are the additions that change conversion for an international customer base.

Crypto

OxaPay, BitPay, Binance Pay and Quidax. Preferred by a real share of hosting and VPN customers, settled without you holding crypto, and not chargeback-able. How crypto acceptance works.

Local methods

iDEAL in the Netherlands, Bizum in Spain, UPI in India, mobile money and USSD across Africa. Each one is the default way to pay somewhere your cards convert badly.

Agreement-capable cards

PayPal, Stripe and Authorize.net among others create a reusable agreement at first payment, which is what lets a renewal charge itself later without the customer present.
